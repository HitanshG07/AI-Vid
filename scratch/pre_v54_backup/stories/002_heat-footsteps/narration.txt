In my town, every footstep glows with your body heat for sixty seconds. Walk, it's faint. Run, it blazes. Blind Striders hunt it.
A jogger sprinted to the bakery. Boom. Striders through the roof.
So we walk slow, in ice boots. Only the night van drives fast. My sister June hugs a hot-water bottle. The laundromat steams all night.
June saw her cat. She ran. Barefoot. Her prints blazed. Sixty seconds. The Striders turned.
I threw ice boots. Hiss! Still bright.
I flung her bottle. Splat! It froze.
I dragged her into the laundromat. Crack! The door split. My prints led in.
I yanked the dryer open. Hot socks on. I stomped. Harder than ever. My trail blazed white. Every Strider followed.
I jumped the night van. Socks onto the roof. They chased it onto the frozen lake. Crack! The ice melted. Every Strider sank.
The driver jumped out. Then he ran. One grey hand burst through the ice. Gone. Only his radio crackled.
My feet burned for weeks. So June carried me home. Slowly.
Then Christmas came. Streets full. Nobody ran. Except June. Every morning, bringing me hot socks.
