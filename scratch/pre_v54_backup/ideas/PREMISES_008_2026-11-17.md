## P1. "The Number Above My Neighbor's Head"
- **Hook (0 to 3 s):** "In this world, everyone has a number over their head: how many people would miss them. Mine says 41. Hers says 0."
- **World rule:** A floating number above every person counts how many people would truly miss them if they vanished.
- **Twist:** The narrator, jealous of the quiet girl next door because she keeps "stealing" his attention, tries to prove her 0 is right. Instead his own number drops one by one each time he's cruel, and hers ticks 0 to 1 the night he admits, out loud and alone, that he would miss her. The number was never about her popularity. It was waiting for him.
- **Meaning:** Jealousy is often love that hasn't admitted itself yet. Being missed starts with one person choosing to care.
- **Ending:** The two sit on the apartment stairs as her number reads 1 and his reads 40, and he says that's the best trade he ever made.
- **Freshness combo:** numbers on people · jealousy→love · apartment block · Standalone.
- **Cast:**
  - Narrator "Theo": R15, red hoodie, black cargo pants, messy brown hair, confident-grin decal turning to guilty.
  - "Iris" (the neighbor): R15, oversized grey cardigan, long black hair, quiet-eyes decal, then small smile.
  - Theo's mom: R15, green sweater, bun; one scene.
  - Extras: 4 or 5 tenants and kids in the hallway, varied catalog outfits.
- **Rule on screen:** A white BillboardGui number above every head in every shot, with a red flash on the tick-down and a gold flash on the tick-up.
- **Sets:** an apartment block hallway (brick, wainscot, doors with numbers), a stairwell, Theo's small kitchen, and a rooftop at night for the admission.

## P2. "My Shadow Leaves When I Don't Forgive"
- **Hook:** "In this town, your shadow walks away from you every time you hold a grudge. On the night bus, I have no shadow left."
- **World rule:** Each unforgiven grudge peels your shadow off and it stands on its own, a few steps further away; with too many, you cast none.
- **Twist:** The narrator learns his estranged father, riding the same last bus, has been followed by *the narrator's* missing shadows for years: the father never let go of the guilt, so they gathered around him. When the narrator forgives him, the shadows walk back across the aisle, and the father finally casts his own again.
- **Meaning:** A grudge doesn't only darken you; it keeps the other person stuck in the dark too. Forgiveness frees two people.
- **Ending:** The bus pulls into the depot at dawn with two clear shadows side by side on the floor.
- **Freshness combo:** light/shadow · forgiveness · bus/train · Standalone.
- **Note vs ledger:** 000 has weighted shadows (cleverness, different rule and meaning). 004 is forgiveness by stones (meaning "forgiveness needs action"). Here the meaning is "forgiveness frees both". The verifier should rule on this overlap; ranked lower for that reason.
- **Cast:** narrator "Jae" (R6, black bomber jacket, beanie, tired decal); his father "Mr. Han" (R6, worn tan coat, grey hair, ashamed decal); bus driver (cap, uniform, extra).
- **Rule on screen:** black, slightly transparent R6 silhouettes (a cloned rig with Transparency 0.4 and black colour) standing apart from their owners, plus a missing floor shadow (lamps placed so the gap reads).
- **Sets:** a night city bus interior (seats, poles, neon strip), a bus stop with a streetlamp, the depot at dawn.
- **Search queries:**

## P3. "Grandma's Coins Count Down"
- **Hook:** "In this village, you're born with 100 gold coins in your chest. Every lie spends one. Grandma's last coin just fell."
- **World rule:** Everyone has a small glass coin jar fixed to their chest (visible); each lie removes a coin, and when the jar is empty your voice turns silent.
- **Twist:** Grandma's jar has been emptying fast and the family thinks she's a liar. The narrator discovers she has been telling exactly one lie a day for years, "I already ate", so the grandkids get her share of food on the farm. Her last coin goes on the final "I'm not hungry". The narrator gives her his first honest act: he tells the whole family the truth, and her coin comes back from his jar to hers.
- **Meaning:** Some lies are the most honest kind of love, and seeing them is how we pay them back.
- **Ending:** At the farm table, the narrator sets down two plates and says "Eat first, Grandma", and her jar holds one shining coin.
- **Freshness combo:** money/value · gratitude · farm · Standalone.
- **Note:** this differs from rejected 003 (the chest lie counter, "radical honesty"). The verifier must check the "lie counter on body" overlap. It's kept because the coin jar, the twist and the meaning are new.
- **Cast:** narrator "Ben" (R15, overalls, straw hat, freckle decal); Grandma "Nana Rose" (R15, floral dress, white bun, warm-wrinkle decal); Mom (apron); little sister "Pip" (pigtails, yellow dress).
- **Rule on screen:** a glass jar on the chest with gold neon coins; a coin pops out with a gold particle when someone lies.
- **Sets:** a farmhouse kitchen (wood planks, warm lamp), the farmyard with a barn and fence, a chicken coop.

## P4. "The Glow You Get for Being Brave" (recommended)
- **Hook:** "In this beach town, the first time you're truly brave, your outline glows gold forever. I'm 16. I'm the only one who's never lit up."
- **World rule:** A person's body gains a permanent gold outline the first time they do something truly brave; everyone in town glows except the narrator.
- **Twist:** In a storm, the narrator's little brother is pulled out by a rip current. The narrator freezes, and the lifeguard captain, the brightest-glowing person in town, swims out and saves him. Later, she admits her glow came the day she called for help when she was too scared to go in herself. The narrator realises courage isn't the absence of fear. At the next rescue the narrator, terrified, doesn't swim; he runs and rings the shore bell no one else noticed, and that is when he lights up.
- **Meaning:** Courage isn't being fearless or heroic; it's doing the right small thing while you're still scared.
- **Ending:** He stands on the pier glowing gold beside his brother, both outlined as the bell still swings.
- **Freshness combo:** emotions made physical · courage · beach town · Standalone (about 165 words).

## P5. "We Sleep Holding the Red Thread"
- **Hook:** "In this hospital, every patient's family ties a red thread to their wrist. When the thread turns white, they're gone. My sister's turned white an hour ago. She's still talking."
- **World rule:** A family-tied red wrist thread shows if a loved one is alive and bonded: it turns white when the bond ends (death).
- **Twist:** The thread turned white because the sister cut the family bond on purpose, so that her parents, who've been sleeping in the ward for months, would grieve early, go home and live. The narrator ties a new thread, his own, and says "you don't get to leave us early". It stays red to the end, and when she does die later, the family is with her.
- **Meaning:** Grief shared beats grief avoided; you can't protect people from loving you.
- **Ending:** Months later, the narrator wears the white thread on his own wrist on the hospital steps, smiling at sunrise.
- **Freshness combo:** family rituals · grief · hospital · Standalone.
- **Note:** heavy tone; a death ending is riskier for retention and platform safety. The "red string of fate" trope is common, so the researcher must search it hard.
- **Cast:** narrator "Leo" (R15, school uniform, short black hair); sister "Ana" (hospital gown, beanie, pale-smile decal); Mom and Dad (casual, tired decals); nurse extra.
- **Rule on screen:** a Neon red (or white) band part welded to every wrist, shown in close-ups.
- **Sets:** a hospital ward (beds, curtains, a window), a corridor, and the steps outside at sunrise.
- **Search queries:**
  1. "in this world a red thread on your wrist turns white when they die"
  2. "hospital red thread turns white sister still alive story"
  3. "roblox story red thread wrist hospital sister"
  4. "sister cuts family bond so parents grieve early short story"
