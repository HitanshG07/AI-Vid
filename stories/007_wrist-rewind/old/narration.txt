In my world, tapping your wrist rewinds any mistake by ten seconds. But your hands age one year every time. Kids rewind dropped phones. Jace rewinds bad selfies; he has thirty-year-old hands. Mine are fifteen. Zero taps. My grandma spent her taps on herself, and her hands shook too hard to hold mine. So I saved mine. Then chemistry. Mia sits beside Jace. She once lent me a pencil when nobody else would talk to me. Jace was showing off. Two bottles, same shape. Red label is acid. Plain one is water. He grabbed the red one. Mia leaned in to watch. He poured. The beaker hissed, then burst, right at her face. I tapped. Ten seconds back, before the pour. I yelled. He poured anyway. Tap. I grabbed his arm. He shoved me, and poured. Tap. My knuckles started to wrinkle. So I stopped fighting and started watching. Water bottle, left of the acid. He pours with his right hand. At second seven, he turns to pose for his phone. Tap. Last loop. I waited. He turned. Flash. I swapped the bottles. He poured water. Nothing happened. Jace laughed and called me boring. I pulled my sleeves over my hands. Then he saw them. "Dude, you're nineteen?" Four taps. Four years. For ten seconds. Mia smiled at me, her face perfect, and lent me a pencil again. Grandma spent her years on her own mistakes. I spent mine on someone else. A few years of my hands for her whole face. They were saved for her.
